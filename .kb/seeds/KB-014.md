## 引言节选
Multi-ﬁngered robotic hands have transitioned from laboratory curiosities to essential components in modern automation
pipelines, including precision assembly, warehouse logistics, and agricultural harvesting [1,2]. Achieving human-level dexterity re-
quires not only sophisticated perception and planning but also real-time optimization of contact forces that guarantee stable grasping
without damaging manipulated objects [3]. In industrial insertion tasks, a ﬁve-ﬁngered hand must modulate ﬁngertip forces to pre-
vent both slip and crushing of delicate components while reacting to external disturbances within millisecond-scale control loops [4].
In agricultural robotics, soft three-ﬁngered grippers must distribute grasping forces uniformly across fragile fruit surfaces to avoid
bruising, necessitating fast re-optimization as the hand traverses a picking trajectory [5,6]. These application scenarios share a com-
mon mathematical backbone: the grasping force optimization (GFO) problem, which seeks the minimal-magnitude contact forces that
satisfy Coulomb friction constraints and equilibrium conditions against time-varying external loads.
 Despite signiﬁcant progress in grasp planning and tactile sensing [7,8], the underlying force allocation problem remains a com-

## 结论节选
A forward-reﬂected-backward algorithm with backtracking stepsize selection is proposed for solving the grasping-force optimiza-
tion problem in multi-ﬁngered robotic hands. The problem is formulated as a convex quadratic circular cone program, providing a
rigorous mathematical framework that incorporates the physical constraints of friction cones and force equilibrium.
 The key theoretical contribution is a complete, self-contained convergence analysis. By transforming the weighted KKT system into
standard 𝑣-coordinates via 𝑣 = 𝑊 𝑢, we reduce the algorithm to an adaptive FRB method with backtracking for an aﬃne monotone
operator. We establish global convergence of the iterates, prove an 𝑂(1∕𝑘) convergence rate for the natural monotone inclusion
residual, and show that once the active contact constraints are identiﬁed (which occurs in ﬁnite iterations), the algorithm reduces
to an unconstrained linear iteration on the free force subspace and attains local R-linear convergence. Furthermore, we prove ﬁnite-
iteration identiﬁcation of the active contact constraints, which explains the rapid practical convergence. A stochastic extension for
online grasping force adaptation under sensor noise is introduced and analyzed.
