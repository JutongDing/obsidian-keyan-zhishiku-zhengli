## 引言节选
Closed-loop adaptive intelligence in robotic hands (i.e. feedback-driven adaptation
 of grasp and in-hand manipulation policies under contact uncertainty) is considered
 a crucial enabler for the next generation of human-centered manufacturing and ser-
 vice robots that operate in shared human–robot workspaces, exhibit a high tolerance
 to product variation and maintain safety and reliability [1, 2]. Dexterous multifingered
 hands are considered to offer a unique path toward dexterous manipulation beyond
 those available from simpler grippers. However, achieving a deployable adaptive behav-
 ior requires codesign at the system level, not merely in one specific layer, but across the
 various components of a system: embodiment (mechanism, actuation, proprioception),
 perception and control/learning [3, 4].

 1.1 Motivation: industry 5.0 and human-centered manufacturing
 In the context of Industry 5.0 (with its focus on human well-being, resilience and sus-
 tainability), the capabilities required of an industrial robot operating alongside people
 are substantially increased. Fixed, programmed trajectories and a low rate of feedback
 exchange, suitable for confined, highly structured work cells, can become a burden in a
 shared workspace where, for example, the geometry of handled objects can vary, the fix-
 tures may become loose or dislocated over time and co-workers might exhibit behaviors
 far less predictable. Practical industrial robot setups today commonly rely on limited
 sensing and simplified end-effectors that restrict the variety of manipulations performed
 [1–6]. A dexterous hand potentially increases the contact richness or safety over a basic
