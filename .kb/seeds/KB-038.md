## 引言节选
Traditional industrial robot manipulators are extensively used to replace humans in performing heavy, repetitive and un-
safe manufacturing tasks [1]. They are usually only able to manipulate known objects in a structured operating environment.
However, in non-manufacturing ﬁelds, such as marine exploitation, space operations, medical, service and entertainment, the
operating environments are usually diverse and complex. This requires a robot to perform increasingly human-like manip-
ulation tasks [2]. Therefore, the demand for robot end-effectors with more ﬂexibility and higher adaptability is tremendous
and sharply increases.
 Inspired by the grasping motions of human hands, dexterous hands (DHs) are designed to have the ability to grasp dif-
ferent types of irregular objects with the use of humanoid ﬁngers. They are also essential elements of the new generation of
social and service robots [3]. Due to these reasons, DHs have attracted broad attention worldwide over the past few decades
[4]. To accomplish grasping actions similar to those of human hands, a variety of fully actuated humanoid DHs have been
developed [5]. Representative DHs include the Stanford/Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) hand [6], the Utah/Massachusetts In-
stitute of Technology (MIT) hand [7], the Cyber hand [8], the DLR hand [9], the DLR hand II [10], and the Robonaut hand

## 结论节选
This study is concerned with the design, analysis and experiment of a low-cost, multimode, easily controlled, and pas-
sively adaptive ﬁve-ﬁngered UADH. The UADH is driven by 6 DC servo motors and its size is equal to that of a human hand.
It can perform various grasping postures, including ﬁngertip grasping, palm-enveloping grasping, and side-contact grasping,
to basically fulﬁll the demands that occur in real applications. With the use of the linkage-slider and rack-pinion mecha-
nisms, the UADH has the ability to powerfully grasp objects with different shapes and sizes. After a detailed presentation
of the mechanical design, the kinematics and statics of the UADH are described successively. In the kinematic analysis, the
ﬁngertip grasping process and the adaptive grasping process are separately described. The coupled motion of ﬁnger pha-
lanxes is evaluated through kinematic simulations. The results indicate that the motion coupled ratios of three phalanxes
are basically consistent with those of a human hand during the entire grasping process. The workspaces of the index ﬁnger
and thumb during the ﬁngertip grasping and adaptive grasping are calculated to demonstrate the operational accessibility
